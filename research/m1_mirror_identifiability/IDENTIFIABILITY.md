# What can and cannot be identified

1. With known reference timing, the two-column matrix `[s,s*]` may have full rank and good conditioning. This permits estimation of its projection coefficients under that model, not physical transmitter/receiver separation.
2. The augmented I/Q matrix has form `[[mu,nu],[nu*,mu*]]`. An admissible invertible augmented matrix Q gives the same cascade through `(Mr Q)(Q^-1 Mt)`. Arbitrary GL(2,C) matrices need not preserve this structure; the ambiguity already exists inside the admissible augmented class.
3. This particular L-STF satisfies `conj(s[n]) = -j*s[n+10]` at 25 MHz. Thus a ten-sample phase change swaps the two columns up to phase. Good two-column condition numbers do not establish joint timing/coefficient uniqueness. Our dominant-direct tie convention selects a descriptor representative; it does not identify the physical arrival phase. Complex q is reference-dependent. This ambiguity is always reported.
4. With unknown frequency-selective channel and zero CFO, ordinary shifted STF columns and their conjugates can span the same twelve occupied tones. A delayed ordinary channel can imitate a conjugate component. The audit reports the rank of a 12-delay ordinary channel and a doubled widely-linear channel. This is another reason not to equate a mirror fit gain with TX hardware.
5. Nonzero CFO can provide additional linear independence under known timing/channel assumptions; it does not by itself remove all cascade, gain, reference or channel ambiguities. Four-column condition/rank is tested at CFO 0, 100, 1000 and 100000 Hz.

The narrow candidate is a gauge-defined **effective observation proxy** with a held-segment prediction test and equal-order in-band control. A source GO would only qualify a separately authorized M1-B probe. A STOP closes this explicit-estimation candidate, not all complex mirror representations. No causal TX/RX decomposition, cross-RX event pairing, hardware calibration or TX separability result is produced here.
